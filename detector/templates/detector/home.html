How it works

One paste runs the whole examination. Nothing about the link leaves the page unexplained.

  1. 01
    Feature extraction

    45 features are measured from the URL string alone: lengths, dots and subdomains, special characters, whether the host is an IP number, punycode, link shorteners, sensitive words, brand names in the wrong place, and the randomness of the domain name.

  2. 02
    Two detectors, side by side

    A transparent rule-based detector scores weighted warning signs, and a Random Forest trained on verified PhishTank URLs and Tranco domains gives a phishing probability. The headline score is the average of the two.

  3. 03
    Explanations, not just a score

    Every triggered rule is listed with its weight, and SHAP attributions from the model are translated into plain sentences — so you learn the warning signs, not just the verdict.

One paste, every check

A single check runs all of these — including the tricks most people have never heard of.

  • The “@” trick

    Everything before @ is ignored; the address you see is a decoy.

  • Typosquat

    A domain one or two letters from a real brand (paypaI, gooogle).

  • Open-redirect laundering

    The link starts on a trusted site but redirects elsewhere.

  • File-extension domains

    invoice.zip looks like a file, but it is a website.

  • Invisible characters

    Hidden letters that change what the address really is.

  • Free-hosting logins

    A fake login page on a platform anyone can publish on.

  • Homoglyphs

    Look-alike letters from another alphabet impersonating a brand.

HTTPS does not mean safe.

Most phishing sites now use HTTPS — a padlock means the connection is encrypted, not that the site is honest. PhishLens treats HTTPS as a weak signal only.
